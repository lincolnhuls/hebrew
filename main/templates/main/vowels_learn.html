Hebrew Vowels at a Glance

Hebrew is known as an abjad language, meaning that it is written with consonants only. However, in modern times, something called the niqqud, or vowel points, have been added to the text to help with pronunciation. When reading, pronounce the letter first, then the vowel sound.

A Note on Long and Short Vowels

In Biblical Hebrew, some vowels are considered “long” and others “short”, and the same mark can sometimes behave differently depending on its position in the word. Those details are important, but they go beyond the scope of this first vowels lesson. For now, focus on recognizing each vowel’s basic sound.

Qamets vs. Qamets Hatuph

Qamets and Qamets Hatuph use the same written mark, but they sound different. Qamets usually sounds like a as in father, while Qamets Hatuph sounds like a short o as in soft. For the most part, when you see either of them, it is safe to assume that it is a Qamets. Only rarely does a true Qamets Hatuph appear.

Why You See a Bet in the Quiz

In the quiz, each vowel is shown together with the consonant Bet (בּ) so that the small vowel marks are easier to see. Think of the Bet as a simple “carrier” letter: it helps you see the vowel clearly, but the quiz is really testing the vowel, not the consonant.
