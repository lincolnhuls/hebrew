{% if letter.name_en == "Aleph" %}Aleph is quite a unique letter in the Aleph-Bet as it does not have a direct comparison to a letter in English. The letter Aleph acts as what is known as a glottal stop. An example of this can be found when someone says "uh-oh." Notice as you say "uh-oh," when you switch from "uh" to "oh", how your throat closes and all the sound stops before resuming with "oh"? That is a glottal stop! It essentially acts as a hard stop in a word. When you see it in Hebrew, it means to put that kind of stop at that point in the word!{% elif letter.name_en == "Bet" %}Bet makes the same sound as B in English. The same as the B in boy!{% elif letter.name_en == "Gimel" %}Gimel makes the same sound as G in English. The same as the G in get!{% elif letter.name_en == "Dalet" %}Dalet makes the same sound as D in English. The same as the V in van!{% elif letter.name_en == "Zayin" %}Zayin makes the same sound as Z in English.
